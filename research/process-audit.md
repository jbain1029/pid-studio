# Process component symbol study

Audit date: 2026-09-12. Read-only examination of the current 141-type `pidcore.CATALOG`, including the applied supplied-legend overlays. Scope: all 45 types in Process Equipment, Vessels & Reactors, Pumps & Compressors, Heat Transfer, Separation & Treatment, and Steam & Utilities. This includes the three relevant Rankine additions. No production code or drawings changed for this study.

## Result and meaning of the classifications

| Outcome | Count | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Supported family | 22 | Recognizable family/selected supplied-reference convention, with coherent displayed nozzles. This is not verification of every subtype or a claim of ISO compliance. |

These counts include implementation integrity, not only resemblance of the standalone icon. In particular, two pump bodies resemble the selected supplied reference but have undersized routing envelopes. Tests passing and `legend_reference` metadata were NOT accepted as visual verification.

## Highest-priority findings

1. **Turbine outlet is visibly detached and shaft is typed as an instrument signal.** `CR`, turbine definition: bottom polygon edge runs from (-30,18) to (30,28), hence its y coordinate at x=0 is 23. The outlet lead begins at (0,28), leaving a five-unit gap. The right-hand `shaft` port is `signal`, even though its name describes mechanical power transmission. Correct the wall contact and introduce an explicit mechanical relationship/shaft convention before treating this as a conventional turbine P&ID component.
2. **Two redraws outgrew their routing envelopes.** `screw_pump` and `reciprocating_pump` are now circles of radius 24 (`LP:60`, `LP:89`, `LP:81`), but their inherited routing half-heights remain 18 and 20. Thus a line considered clear by the router can overlap the visible body. Preserve a deliberate nozzle/body envelope, not a stale pre-redraw rectangle. This is an app integrity mismatch, not a condemnation of the selected circle symbols.
3. **Open and floating-roof tanks still have floating vent stubs.** `CP:103`, `CP:107`: the top `vent` nozzle is drawn in empty space, disconnected from the open tank/roof. An open surface is not itself a pipe connection. Do not silently repair by converting the vent to a dip pipe: that changes the service meaning. Select a genuine vent/roof nozzle treatment or omit/disable the port through a deliberate document migration.
4. **The blower label asserts a centrifugal subtype while the graphic is a generic propeller/fan pictogram.** `CP:141`; compare D Figure 15 and L Pumps/Centrifuges fan variants. Either choose a centrifugal casing/tangential discharge convention or use a generic fan label with the type specified in properties.
5. **Surface heating coil uses `signal` to represent a mounting relationship.** `CR`, `heating_coil`: one left-hand `mount` port is `signal`; the right end of the coil is unconnected. This can be a legitimate project-specific attachment annotation, but should not be represented as a fluid heating coil or measurement/control signal without a dedicated relationship convention. It has no two-port heating-fluid circuit or electrical supply definition.

## Circuit and nozzle interpretation cautions

- The current **generic exchanger** has a continuous process zigzag; the previous detached-tip defect is no longer present. Its utility ports contact the shell top/bottom. Do not repeat the stale finding.
- The current **shell-and-tube exchanger** maps the two process ports to left/right ends of a continuous zigzag, and utility ports to shell top/bottom using external adapters. Those adapters do not intersect one another. It is a coherent generic exchanger convention, not a depiction of a specific TEMA construction or nozzle schedule.
- **Double-pipe exchanger** now has open central tube ends: process ports at y=0 enter the inner bore, utility ports at y=-15 and +15 enter the upper/lower annulus representations. The old "all four ports enter annulus" finding is obsolete. However, the drawing shows upper/lower annulus as separate strips. They can be connected around the tube out of the drawing plane in a cross-sectional interpretation, but that convention is not self-evident. This is an adaptation requiring a legend, not demonstrated mixing or a proven process blockage. Prefer the reference's coherent double-pipe/hairpin depiction, or explicitly identify tube/annulus service and explain the sectional representation.
- **Plate exchanger** has four contacts to an abstract plate-pack boundary; diagonal process pairings are defined by port names, not geometrically recoverable internal paths. A shows that real plate-unit arrangements vary with pass configuration. A generic symbol may omit internal detail, but its port pairing should be available in the legend/properties/AI contract and should not be described as a universal physical nozzle arrangement.
- **Jacketed reactor** is intentionally composite. Process feed reaches the inner wall and product reaches the inner bottom; jacket services contact the right-side jacket. The vent has moved away from the drive. Those earlier defects are repaired. The left and right jacket strips are not explicitly connected to one another, and the feed crosses the left jacket outline without a penetration convention. Treat the strips as a jacket indication, not a literal independent circuit schematic; document this or improve nozzle penetration depiction.
- **Air-blown cooler** is now a bundle-style body with fan beneath, rather than a disconnected serpentine coil. Both process nozzles reach its body. The earlier detached-coil finding no longer applies.
- **Boiler and fired-heater** boundary contacts are package-level service declarations, not a fully drawn burner/water/steam topology. The boiler has no flue port; this is not proof of an impossible boiler, but it cannot serve as a complete boiler subsystem drawing without added details.
